# How decisions about buses get made

This page explains who decides what about buses in Lawrence, when money decisions happen, and how anyone can comment or attend. It does not suggest what to ask for.

## Who does what

| Who | What they decide or do |
|---|---|
| **Lawrence City Commission** (elected) | Adopts the city budget each year, including the transit fund, and approves major contracts such as the bus operations contract. Meets the first, second and third Tuesday of each month, usually at 5:45 PM at City Hall, 6 E. 6th St.[c1] |
| **Lawrence Transit** (a city department) | Plans city bus routes and schedules, runs a yearly route review with a public survey, and manages the contractor that operates the buses. In 2026 the survey ran March 4 to April 3, and changes started August 1.[c2] |
| **KU (University of Kansas)** | Pays for KU routes ("KU On Wheels") from student fees set by the student senate. KU and the city have separate budgets and contracts, and two shared routes.[c3] |
| **Lawrence-Douglas County MPO** (Metropolitan Planning Organization, the regional transportation planning group) | Keeps the long-range transportation plan and the TIP (Transportation Improvement Program), the list of projects that use federal money. Its Policy Board meets the third Thursday of each month at 3 PM at City Hall.[c1] |
| **KDOT** (Kansas Department of Transportation) | Gives state transit money (about $1.3 million a year to Lawrence Transit) and adds approved local projects to the statewide project list (the STIP), which has its own comment period.[c4] |
| **FTA** (Federal Transit Administration) | Sends federal formula money each year (about $3.8 to $4.0 million expected for Lawrence) and runs competitive grants, mostly for buses and facilities.[c4] |
| **Voters** | Approve or reject local transit sales taxes. |

## The yearly budget calendar (2027 budget example)

- **Spring:** the city collects community feedback (April 20 to May 1, 2026) and the Commission discusses priorities (May and June).[c5]
- **July 7:** the City Manager presents the proposed budget and the 5-year capital plan.[c5]
- **July 14:** the Commission sets the maximum tax rate for the public notice.[c5]
- **September 1:** public hearings on the budget and the property tax rate.[c5]
- **September 15:** the Commission adopts the budget. It is filed with the County Clerk by October 1, as state law requires.[c5]

## How to comment or attend

- **City Commission:** attend in person at City Hall or online by Zoom (the link is in each agenda). Speakers usually get three minutes. Written comments go to ccagendas@lawrenceks.gov by noon on the meeting day. You can also email all commissioners at commissioners@lawrenceks.gov.[c1]
- **Agendas and videos:** posted on the city's agenda site, [lawrenceks.civicweb.net](https://lawrenceks.civicweb.net/Portal/Default.aspx).[c1]
- **MPO plans:** each new TIP has a 30-day comment period, and each change has a 15-day period. Written comments go to mpo@lawrenceks.gov.[c6]
- **Lawrence Transit:** watch for the yearly route survey, or contact Lawrence Transit through the public channels listed on lawrencetransit.org.[c3]

## The November 3, 2026 ballot question

Lawrence voters will decide whether to repeal the current 0.2% sales tax for public transit and replace it with a 0.3% sales tax for ten years. If it passes, the new tax would be collected from April 1, 2027 to March 31, 2037. If it does not pass, the 0.2% tax continues until March 31, 2029.[c3] This site takes no position.
