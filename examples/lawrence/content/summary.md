# Bus access in Lawrence: a one-page summary

This page is a short, plain-language version of a longer report. It does not ask anyone to support or oppose any plan or vote. Every number comes from public data, and the full report shows where each one comes from.

## What we looked at

We asked a simple question: **if you don't drive, can you get to the places you need by walking and taking the bus?** We checked how long it takes to reach a grocery store, a doctor and a pharmacy, and how many jobs you can reach. We checked weekday mornings, evenings, Saturdays and Sundays. We used the real bus schedule, real streets and U.S. Census numbers for every neighborhood in Douglas County.

A trip "counts" if it takes 30 minutes or less, door to door, including the walk and the wait.

## Key findings

1. **About half of homes can't reach all three basics by bus in 30 minutes.** On a weekday morning, about 54% of households in Douglas County live where a grocery store, a doctor or a pharmacy is more than 30 minutes away by walking and bus. For homes without a car, it is about 39%. These numbers depend on how far people are assumed to walk: with a faster walk the share is about 40%, and if no one walks more than 10 minutes at a time it is about 79%.
2. **It is hardest outside central Lawrence.** Homes near downtown and KU have the best access. Homes in Eudora, Baldwin City and the countryside are not near any Lawrence city bus stop.
3. **Nights and Sundays are much harder.** On a weekday morning, the average home can reach about 19% of the county's jobs within 45 minutes. At 9 PM that falls to 10%. On Sunday, when city buses don't run on fixed routes, it is 9%.
4. **More frequent buses help the most people for the money.** Running buses twice as often on a few busy routes gave the most homes new 30-minute access for each dollar spent. Adding Sunday or later evening buses mostly helps people reach more jobs.
5. **Some important facts are not public.** Real-time bus locations are public, but how many people ride each bus, and how full buses get, are not. A public-records request for this data was filed on October 24, 2026.

6. **Compared with other college towns,** Lawrence's city buses run more hours per person than some (Columbia, Lincoln, Manhattan) and fewer than others (Ames, Champaign-Urbana). See the [peer towns page](/peers.html).

**How sure are we?** We re-ran the numbers with different walking speeds, walking limits and times of day. The exact shares change, mostly with walking assumptions, but the pattern stays the same. Nights and Sundays are always harder, and central Lawrence always has the best access. See the [full report's appendix](/#appendix-how-sturdy-are-these-numbers).

## What options exist, and what they might cost

These are **examples to compare**, not plans or promises. Costs are rough. "Per household" means the yearly cost divided by the number of homes that would newly reach all three basics within 30 minutes. A range shows two cases. In the first, the city pays it all. In the second, outside money pays half. Today there is no outside money set aside for extra bus hours, so the first case is more likely.

| Example option | Rough cost each year | Cost per household that gains 30-minute access, each year |
|---|---|---|
| Buses twice as often on Route 5 | about $420,000 | $190 to $380 |
| Buses twice as often on Routes 1, 5 and 9 | about $1.25 million | $290 to $570 |
| Sunday buses, 10 AM to 4 PM | about $340,000 | $1,180 to $2,360 |
| Weekday buses until about 10 PM | about $1 million | $1,740 to $3,480 |
| A new bus stop (one time) | $1,150 to $49,500 | Few homes gain; see the stops page |

Lawrence's local bus money comes mostly from a city sales tax. On **November 3, 2026**, Lawrence voters will decide whether to replace the current 0.2% transit sales tax with a 0.3% tax for ten years. If it does not pass, the 0.2% tax continues until March 2029. This site takes no position on the vote.
