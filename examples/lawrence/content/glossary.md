# Glossary and FAQ

## Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Access (30-minute access)** | Whether you can get somewhere within 30 minutes, door to door, by walking and bus, including waiting. |
| **ACS (American Community Survey)** | A yearly U.S. Census Bureau survey. It estimates things like how many homes have no car. Because it is a survey, each number has a margin of error. |
| **Block group** | A small Census area, usually 600 to 3,000 people. It is the smallest area with survey data on cars and income. |
| **Census tract** | A larger Census area made of a few block groups, usually 1,200 to 8,000 people. |
| **Capital cost** | A one-time cost to build or buy something, like a bus, a shelter or a concrete pad. |
| **Operating cost** | The ongoing cost of running service: drivers, fuel and repairs, paid every year. |
| **Dwell time** | The time a bus spends stopped at a stop while people get on and off. |
| **Fixed route** | A bus that follows the same path and schedule every day. |
| **On Demand** | Lawrence Transit's book-a-ride service for nights and Sundays (Sundays 8 a.m.-6 p.m. and nights 8 p.m.-6 a.m., $3 since Mar 1, 2026, within city limits; confirmed by Lawrence Transit Operations Manager Jared Allen, email 2026-10-27), which picks you up within a zone instead of following a fixed path. |
| **Frequency / headway** | How often a bus comes. "Headway" is the time between buses. A 30-minute headway means a bus every 30 minutes. |
| **GTFS** | A standard file format for bus schedules (routes, stops and times). Map apps use it. We used Lawrence Transit's and KU's public GTFS files. |
| **GTFS Realtime** | A live feed of where buses are right now. |
| **LODES** | Census data that counts jobs by where they are located. |
| **Margin of error (±)** | How far a survey estimate could be from the true number. "54% ± 2%" means the true value is probably between 52% and 56%. |
| **MPO** | Metropolitan Planning Organization: the regional group that plans transportation and lists projects that use federal money. |
| **Revenue hours / service hours** | Hours that buses are out carrying riders. More service hours cost more money. |
| **Scenario** | A "what if" test. We edit a copy of the schedule and measure what changes. It is not a plan. |
| **TIP** | Transportation Improvement Program: the MPO's list of funded transportation projects for the next few years. |
| **Federal / local match** | Grants usually pay part of a cost (for example 80%), and the city pays the rest (the "match"). |

## Frequently asked questions

**Who made this site?**
An independent analysis. It is not run by the city, Lawrence Transit, KU or any campaign. It does not take a side on any plan or vote.

**Is this an official city report?**
No. It uses public data from the city, Lawrence Transit, the U.S. Census Bureau and federal transit records. Each source is listed on the [sources page](/sources.html).

**Why 30 minutes?**
It is a simple, round limit that keeps a trip to the store or doctor within about an hour round trip. The full report also shows results for 20, 45 and 60 minutes.

**Does this count KU buses?**
Yes. The schedule data we used includes the KU routes as well as the city routes.

**Does it count On Demand rides?**
No. On Demand trips are booked and do not have a fixed schedule, so they cannot be timed the same way. The report notes this as a limit. The city's Transit Operations Manager confirmed the published On Demand hours, $3 fare and city-limits coverage on 2026-10-27.

**Why are some numbers given as ranges?**
Census numbers come from a survey and have a margin of error. Costs depend on rates that change over time, so they are shown as low and high estimates.

**What happens if a number is wrong?**
Corrections are listed on the [amendments page](/amendments.html) with the old and new values. You can report a problem with the [feedback form](/feedback.html).

**Does this site say how to vote on November 3?**
No. It describes the ballot question as written and takes no position.
